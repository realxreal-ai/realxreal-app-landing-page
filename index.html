Stop trying to verify people the old way.

You already have instincts for this. They don't hold up anymore.

Improvised verification questions

"Prove it. What did we talk about last time?" Works right up until someone's scraped enough from your social media to answer it too.

Second-guessing real messages

An email or text from someone you actually know, and you still pause — because you genuinely can't tell anymore.

A background hum of dread about cloned voices

Not a plan. Just a worry that gets louder every time a new deepfake story breaks.

Even if you already have a family codeword —

Here's why it breaks the moment you actually need it.

What happens when you forget it?

Mid-panic, at 2am, you're trying to recall a word from six months ago — not use one your phone already remembers for you.

Who says it first?

Whoever speaks first can't prove anything. You're both just trusting that the other person went first — which means neither of you actually proved anything.

What if the cloned voice just asks for it?

A convincing enough clone can ask a panicked family member for the word directly — and get it, before anyone thinks to be suspicious.

Why does everyone share the same word?

Your coworker, your mom, and your college roommate don't need — or deserve — the same secret.

A codeword only works if it changes constantly, lives on the device instead of in memory, and is different for every person you trust. That's the whole idea behind realxreal.

Every trusted contact gets a secure rotating codeword pair. If they don't match, it's not them.
